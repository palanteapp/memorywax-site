Add a record, however you can

Point the camera at a barcode, a center label, the deadwax or the cover. Or type it in. Memory Wax finds the release, and tells you plainly how sure it is.

Five ways to scan

The Add tab has four scan modes and a Listen button. Pick the one that matches what’s in front of you. Barcode, Label and Cover can start reading by themselves once the camera sees something steady. Deadwax always waits for the shutter button. Listen uses the microphone instead of the camera.

ModeWhat it readsBest for
BarcodeThe number under the bars (EAN and UPC)Records from the 1980s on, and most reissues
LabelThe printed center label: label name, catalog number, side, artist and titleOlder records, and records with no barcode
DeadwaxThe matrix etched into the smooth ring by the labelNaming the exact pressing
CoverThe words printed on the front coverWhen the label is hard to read
ListenThe song playing in the room, named by Apple’s ShazamRecords you can’t read, or when you just want to know what’s on. More on Listen
The reading happens on your phone. Apple’s Vision reads the text and the barcode on the device. The camera picture is never saved or uploaded. When a barcode is read, only the number goes to a public music database to look up the release.

Reading the deadwax

This is what sets Memory Wax apart. Two records can share a cover and a barcode and still be different pressings. The difference is etched in the runout.

Deadwax mode turns on the torch, takes six frames over a few seconds, reads each one twice (once with the contrast stretched, once flattened to bring out shallow etching) and votes across the reads. It then explains the marks it recognises, such as RVG for Van Gelder or a known pressing plant, as “a reading, not a fact”.

If you add a free Discogs token in Settings, the scan also compares your etching with the runouts Discogs lists for that catalog number, and shows how many parts match. That match is the only way a record becomes Confirmed.

A helpful trick: light from the side, phone held low on the opposite side, and tilt slowly. The app has a short guide, “How to shoot the deadwax”, behind the “?” button.

Or just type it

“Type it” takes an artist and title, and optionally label, year, catalog number and size (12-inch, 7-inch, 10-inch). Memory Wax asks MusicBrainz, Apple’s public music catalog and, if you added a token, Discogs, then ranks the answers by how well they match. For a 45, type either side.

Bring a list you already keep

From Settings you can import your own Discogs collection (read with your own token, nothing is written back) or upload a CSV file. The Discogs guide walks through it.

How sure are we?

Three honest labels

Every record carries one of these, and Memory Wax never promotes a guess to a fact.

● ConfirmedThe etched matrix was read and it matches the runout listed for one pressing. A strong match, not a guarantee.
◐ LikelyThe cover or barcode points to one pressing, and it’s probably right, but nothing we saw rules out another.
○ Album onlyWe know the album, not which pressing. Scan the deadwax whenever you want to pin it down.

These describe how sure the match is. They’re not a promise about condition or authenticity. Tap “How we know” on any record to see the evidence behind its label.

When there’s more than one

We won’t guess which one you have

If several releases share a barcode or a catalog number, Memory Wax shows them side by side and highlights what differs: catalog number, country, year. You pick yours. If nothing matches, you can add the record exactly as you typed it, and nothing is made up.

Already own it? Memory Wax doesn’t add a duplicate. It adds the new evidence to the record you have.
