Features / The record page

Who made it, and which one is yours

Tap any record for its page: the exact pressing, the players, the engineer, the studio, the story, and your own notes about your copy.

The pressing, with its evidence

The top of the page shows the cover, a seal (Confirmed, Likely, Album only), and a table: label, catalog number, country, matrix, format. If there’s no matrix yet, a button says “Scan the deadwax” and takes you straight to it for this record.

“How we know” opens the evidence, step by step: the barcode that matched, the label that was read, the deadwax and how many parts matched the listed runout, or “Typed by you. Nothing was matched or guessed.” Every claim carries its source.

The session: who played on it

Credits come from MusicBrainz, the open music database, and from Discogs when you’ve added a token. You see the musicians with their instruments, the producer, the engineer and the studio. Memory Wax says how complete it is: “Credits for all 6 tracks” or “Credits on 4 of 6 tracks. The rest aren’t entered yet.”

Every name is a door. Tap one for a short biography (from Wikipedia, only when it’s clearly the right person), the records they’re credited on, and who they played alongside. Records you own are marked, and you can save the rest to your Dream Crate. A trail at the top lets you step back, or swipe in from the left edge.

The story

When a source has it: a short summary from the album’s Wikipedia article, when and where it was recorded, who produced and engineered it, and “Keep digging” buttons for the people involved. Original liner notes are copyrighted, so Memory Wax links to them instead of copying them.

My copy

“The pressing is the record. This part is yours.” It’s private. It lives on your devices, in your own private iCloud, and in the backup files you save. It never goes to anyone else.

  • Condition: M, NM, VG+, VG or G, with a plain explanation of each. Skip it if you’re not sure.
  • A plan for this copy: keeping it, open to trade, or for sale (with an asking price). It’s a private note for you. Nothing is shown to anyone.
  • What you paid and where you got it. Always private. They never show on any shelf.
  • Spins: “Played 5 times”, with a button, “I played this”, for the ones Listen didn’t hear.
  • Photos of your copy: the sleeve, the inner sleeve, the label. Stored only for you.
  • A memory: where you found it, who gave it to you, what you were doing the first time you heard it.
  • A cover photo, if the album art didn’t load or you want your own.

Play it

The Apple Music button plays the album in the app’s own player, using the Apple Music account already on your phone. Memory Wax never asks for your Apple ID and never stores audio. The player shows who played on each track while it plays. Finding albums and showing track lists needs no subscription; playing full songs does. The album Apple plays is its streaming release, which may not be the exact pressing on your shelf, and the app says so.

You can also save a record, shelf or session as a playlist in your Apple Music library. It’s private to you until you choose to share it from Apple’s Music app.
