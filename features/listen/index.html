Features / Listen

Put a record on. It names the song.

Hold your phone near the speaker and tap Listen. Memory Wax names the song, finds it on your shelf, and shows who played on it.

What happens when you tap Listen

Listen uses Apple’s Shazam. The sound becomes a short fingerprint, Shazam matches it, and no recording is kept. It listens only while the Listen sheet is open and only after you tap the button, for up to 15 seconds at a time.

  • If the song is on a record you own, you see “On your shelf”, with where the needle is: side, track, about how long is left, and when to flip. Tap it to open the record. The people credited on that track appear below.
  • If it isn’t, Memory Wax finds the album the song first came out on, shows who played on it, and offers to add it.
  • Every record this song is on. One tap lists the albums, singles, compilations and live records the song appears on, with year and whether it’s on vinyl, and marks the ones you own.

Follow along

Turn on “Follow along” and Memory Wax listens again when the song should be ending, names the next one, and keeps going. When the last song of a side should be over, it taps you on the wrist: “Side A is done. Time to flip to side B.” Keep the Listen sheet open while you do; closing it stops following.

Original or reissue?

Shazam often names a remaster. Memory Wax strips tags like “Remastered” and “Deluxe” before matching, and then does something useful: it compares what you heard with the tracklist of the pressing you picked. If a song isn’t on it, or the songs come in a different order, it says so: “Your copy might be a different version.”

“Find my version” searches every release of that album and shows the ones that have what you heard. Tap yours to switch. Your first pick stays in the record’s evidence, along with a note that you checked by ear. If your deadwax already matched the pressing, Memory Wax trusts that and says the tracklist it has may be incomplete.

What it saves

  • A spin on your copy. When Listen names a song from a record you own, that record gets one spin (at most once every 40 minutes). Spins show as “Played N times” and feed Your year.
  • A session. Every named song goes into the current listening session.
Needs: the iPhone or iPad app and microphone permission. Finding songs and showing who played on them needs no subscription. Playing full songs in the app needs an Apple Music subscription (see the record page).

Private by design

It names the recording, not your pressing

Shazam hears a song, not a specific pressing, so Memory Wax treats what you heard as one more piece of evidence. It never changes a record on its own, and it never records you.
