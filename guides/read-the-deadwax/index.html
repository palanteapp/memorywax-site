Read the deadwax

The smooth band between the last groove and the label carries marks etched at the plant. They’re what tell one pressing from another.

Before you start. To match your etching against the runouts Discogs lists for each pressing, add a free Discogs token first (how to get one). Without it, Memory Wax still reads the marks and explains what it recognises, but it can’t mark a record Confirmed.
  1. Choose a record to attach it toOpen the record’s page and tap “Scan the deadwax” in the Pressing table. The app then knows which record the etching is for. Or open Add, choose Deadwax and scan first, then pick the record from the list.
  2. Set up the lightThis is the whole trick. Turn the record so the runout faces a lamp or window, and hold the phone low on the other side. Light that rakes across the surface makes shallow etching show. Keep the label out of the frame.
  3. Hold the phone 6 to 8 inches awayTilt it about 30° and keep steady. Try not to cover the ring with your thumb.
  4. Tap the round buttonThe torch comes on. Memory Wax takes six frames over a few seconds, reading each one twice, and votes across the reads.
  5. Read the resultYou’ll see the matrix in large type and one of:
    • Matches a listed pressing. At least 80% of the parts match a runout Discogs lists. The record becomes Confirmed.
    • Close to a known pressing. At least half matched. It’s saved as Likely.
    • Matrix read. It read something but couldn’t match it. Saved as Likely.
  6. Save itTap “Save to” the record (or choose it from the list). The matrix is saved with the record, along with how it was matched.

If it didn’t read

  • “Nothing readable yet.” Change the angle of the light. A lamp low and to the side works better than overhead light.
  • “Turn the record a quarter and read more.” Adds a second pass and pools both. Do it two or three times for faint etching.
  • “None of the listed pressings carry this runout.” Often an unlisted variant, not a wrong read. Save it as Likely.
  • Still stuck? “Search the web for this runout” opens a web search for the etching and artist.

What the marks mean

After a read, “What the marks mean” explains the ones it recognises, for example RVG for Rudy Van Gelder, plant and cutting-room codes, and some label prefixes. It’s labelled “a reading, not a fact”, because etching is hand-done and varies.
