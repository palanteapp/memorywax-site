Guides / Scan a barcode

Scan a barcode

The quickest way to add a record from the 1980s on, and most reissues.

  1. Open Add and choose BarcodeIt’s the first mode along the top. Tap the round button. The first time, the phone asks to use the camera. Choose “Allow camera”.
  2. Hold the barcode in the boxAbout six inches away, steady. Barcode mode starts reading by itself when it sees the code. If you turned off “Read automatically” in Settings, tap the round button instead.
  3. Check the resultYou see the cover, title, artist, year, label, catalog number and country, with a note on whether the barcode is listed for vinyl. A barcode can belong to the CD or a reissue, so the app says when that might be the case.
  4. Add itTap “Add to my collection”. The record page opens.

If several releases share the barcode

The app won’t guess. You get a list, with what differs between them highlighted: catalog number, country, year. Compare with your copy and tap yours. Whichever you pick stays at “Release found” until the label or the deadwax narrows it down.

If it won’t read

  • More light, less glare. Barcodes on shrink-wrap and glossy stickers reflect.
  • Tap “Type it” and enter the digits under the bars, usually 12 or 13 numbers. Under 8 digits is too few.
  • No barcode at all? Most 45s and most records before about 1980 never had one. Use Label mode or type the artist and title.
  • “No match yet” means the barcode isn’t in the public database. It isn’t your fault. Try the label or deadwax.
What leaves your phone: only the barcode number, sent to Memory Wax’s record index (and to MusicBrainz if the index has no answer) to look up the release. The camera picture stays on your phone.
