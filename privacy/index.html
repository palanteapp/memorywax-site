Privacy

Privacy policy

Memory Wax · last updated 19 October 2026. In plain words: your collection stays with you, there are no ads and no tracking, and nothing is sold.

Memory Wax helps you find out which pressing of a record you’re holding, shows who made it, and keeps your collection. This page says what the app keeps, what leaves your phone, who sees it, and how to remove it.

What stays on your phone

  • Your records, shelves, grades, notes, what you paid, where you found a record, and photos of your copies.
  • The camera picture. The app reads barcodes, labels and deadwax etching on your phone with Apple’s own text and barcode readers. The picture isn’t uploaded or kept.
  • A copy of your collection (no photos) in the app’s folder in the Files app, included in iCloud Backup if you use it, and a copy made before each update so you can go back.

What goes to your private iCloud

If “Sync with iCloud” is on (the default), your collection data goes to your own private iCloud database, so your iPhone and iPad match. That includes your records, grades, prices paid and places, plans, shelves, Dream Crate, playlists, rooms, listening sessions, your name, photo and answers, and the covers you photographed. Only your Apple Account can read it, and we can’t see it. It doesn’t include photos of your copies, your Discogs token or your community sign-in. You can turn sync off in Settings.

What leaves your phone, and where it goes

Only when you use a feature, and only what it needs.

ServiceWhat is sent
Memory Wax’s own record index (a small server we run on Fly.io)Barcodes, catalog numbers, artist and title words, the text of a deadwax etching, and release or person IDs, to find pressings and credits. Lookups are not logged and the catalogue holds only public data. Like any web service it can see your internet address while it answers. One exception, and only when you tap Report this etching on a record: it keeps the Discogs release number and the etching text, with a one-way code made from your Memory Wax account so one person counts once. Not your name, email, collection or location. You can take your reports back in the app (Account), and deleting your account removes them.
MusicBrainz and the Cover Art ArchiveBarcodes, catalog numbers, label names, artist and title words, and release IDs, to find releases, credits, versions and covers.
Discogs (only if you add a token)Your token with each request, plus catalog numbers, artist and title words and song titles; it also reads your collection list. Nothing is written to Discogs. The token stays on your phone, and in backup files you save.
Apple: music search, Apple Music, ShazamArtist and title words to Apple’s public search; album and song searches and playlists through your own Apple Music account; and, only while you’ve tapped Listen, a short fingerprint of the sound near the phone. No recording is sent or kept. Apple’s privacy policy applies.
Wikipedia and WikidataAlbum titles, artist names and IDs, when you open a record story or a person’s page. Results are kept on your phone for 30 days.
GoogleOnly if you tap “Search the web for this runout”: the matrix text and artist, in a Google search. The app’s typefaces also load from Google Fonts.
Anyone you share withWhatever you choose to share: a card, a song list, an invite link, a backup or CSV file.

We don’t send these services your name, location, collection, prices or photos.

Community accounts (only if you make one)

An account is optional and is only for adding friends. If you make one:

  • Sign-in. With Apple, we receive Apple’s signed token and, the first time, your name if you share it. With email, we receive your email address. Email is used only to sign you in and never shows on your profile. There are no passwords.
  • Your profile is stored on a Memory Wax server: your username, your name, a small copy of your photo, and three short answers (where you’re from, what you spin, favorite album). Friends and people who find your username can see it.
  • Friend requests, friendships, blocks and reports. A report holds the reason and any note you add. The person you report isn’t told who reported them.
  • Invites. An invite link carries your name, username and answers after the “#” in the link. Browsers don’t send that part to the website’s server.
  • Your collection is not sent to the server. Prices and places never go to the community server.

The account service is hosted by Supabase, and sign-in emails are sent through Resend. The website memorywax.app is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like most hosts, they may keep ordinary network logs, such as your IP address, which we don’t see.

What we don’t do

  • No advertising. No analytics or tracking code in the app. We don’t sell data.
  • We don’t collect your location. The “Remember where and when I scan” setting records nothing today.

Your choices

  • Take it with you: Settings → “Download a backup” (everything, with photos) or “Export as CSV”. A backup file contains your prices, places and, if you added one, your Discogs token, so treat it as private.
  • Turn off sync: Settings → “Sync with iCloud”.
  • Pause all sharing: Settings → “Pause all sharing”.
  • Delete your community account: Community account → “Delete my account” removes your sign-in, username, profile, friendships, blocks and the reports you filed from the server for good.
  • Erase this phone: Settings → “Erase everything on this phone” removes your records, photos and tokens, and signs you out. Sync turns off on that phone and your other devices keep their records. Deleting the app removes its data from the phone, but not what’s in your iCloud.
  • Camera and microphone: you can refuse either. You can type a barcode or search by name instead, and Listen simply won’t work.

Children

Memory Wax isn’t for children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect their data.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, we’ll say so in the app and update the date above. Before anything new is collected, such as friends seeing your shelves, we’ll update this page first.

Contact
