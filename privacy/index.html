Privacy policy

Memory Wax · last updated 23 October 2026. In plain words: your collection stays with you, there are no ads and no tracking, and nothing is sold.

Memory Wax helps you find out which pressing of a record you’re holding, shows who made it, and keeps your collection. This page says what the app keeps, what leaves your phone, who sees it, and how to remove it.

What stays on your phone

  • Your records, shelves, grades, notes, what you paid, where you found a record, and photos of your copies.
  • The camera picture. The app reads barcodes, labels and deadwax etching on your phone with Apple’s own text and barcode readers. The picture isn’t uploaded or kept.
  • A copy of your collection (no photos) in the app’s folder in the Files app, included in iCloud Backup if you use it, and a copy made before each update so you can go back.

What goes to your private iCloud

If “Sync with iCloud” is on (the default), your collection data goes to your own private iCloud database, so your iPhone and iPad match. That includes your records, grades, prices paid and places, plans, shelves, Dream Crate, playlists, rooms, listening sessions, your name, photo and answers, and the covers you photographed. Only your Apple Account can read it, and we can’t see it. It doesn’t include photos of your copies, your Discogs token or your community sign-in. You can turn sync off in Settings.

What leaves your phone, and where it goes

Only when you use a feature, and only what it needs.

ServiceWhat is sent
Memory Wax’s own record index (a small server we run on Fly.io)Barcodes, catalog numbers, artist and title words, the text of a deadwax etching, and release or person IDs, to find pressings and credits. Lookups are not logged and the catalogue holds only public data. The server also makes a public web page for each pressing (catalogue details and etchings, no personal data, no cookies, no tracking). Like any web service it can see your internet address while it answers. One exception, and only when you tap Report this etching on a record: it keeps the Discogs release number and the etching text, with a one-way code made by the account service so one person counts once. The index never receives your sign-in, account number, name, email, collection or location: the app asks the account service for a signed ticket that is good for ten minutes and carries only that code. You can take your reports back in the app (Account), and deleting your account removes them.
MusicBrainz and the Cover Art ArchiveBarcodes, catalog numbers, label names, artist and title words, and release IDs, to find releases, credits, versions and covers.
Discogs (only if you add a token)Your token with each request, plus catalog numbers, artist and title words and song titles; it also reads your collection list. Nothing is written to Discogs. The token stays on your phone only; it is never put into a backup file.
Apple: music search, Apple Music, ShazamArtist and title words to Apple’s public search; album and song searches and playlists through your own Apple Music account; and, only while you’ve tapped Listen, a short fingerprint of the sound near the phone. No recording is sent or kept. Apple’s privacy policy applies.
Wikipedia and WikidataAlbum titles, artist names and IDs, when you open a record story or a person’s page. Results are kept on your phone for 30 days.
GoogleOnly if you tap “Search the web for this runout”: the matrix text and artist, in a Google search.
Anyone you share withWhatever you choose to share: a card, a song list, an invite link, a backup or CSV file.

We don’t send these services your name, location, collection, prices or photos. The app and this website serve their own typefaces, so no one’s address goes to Google to draw a page.

Our website and email list

This website sets no cookies and has no analytics or ads. If you sign up on it for updates or the beta test, we collect your email address, whether you asked for updates, the beta test or both, and, only if you choose to fill it in, what kind of records you collect. These go to Buttondown, the service we use to send our emails, which can see your internet address when you sign up and may ask you to confirm your address by email.

We use them only to write to you about Memory Wax and, if you asked, to invite you to test it through Apple’s TestFlight. We don’t sell or share the list. Every email has an unsubscribe link, and you can ask us to delete your details at any time at info@memorywax.app. Buttondown’s own privacy policy applies to the information it holds for us.

Community accounts (only if you make one)

An account is optional and is only for adding friends. If you make one:

  • Sign-in. With Apple, we receive Apple’s signed token and, the first time, your name if you share it. With email, we receive your email address. Email is used only to sign you in and never shows on your profile. There are no passwords.
  • Your profile is stored on a Memory Wax server: your username, your name, a small copy of your photo, and three short answers (where you’re from, what you spin, favorite album). Friends see all of it. You are private by default: nobody can find you until you turn on “Let people find me”. Then someone who types your exact username sees only your username and name, and your photo only if you also turn on “Show my photo to people who find me”. There is no list to browse, and each account can make 30 searches an hour. If you send someone a friend request, they can see your profile so they can decide, but you can’t see theirs until they accept. A request is capped at 50 waiting at a time.
  • Friend requests, friendships, blocks and reports. A report holds the reason, any note you add, and both usernames, and it reaches us by email (sent through Resend) so we can act within about a day. The person you report isn’t told who reported them. We keep the report even if either account is deleted, so evidence can’t be erased: handled reports are removed after one year, or 90 days after either person deleted their account, whichever is sooner.
  • Terms and age. When you make an account you confirm you are 13 or older and agree to the Terms of Use. We store which version you agreed to and when. A suspended account is hidden from other people and can’t add friends; we store that it is suspended and why.
  • Invites. An invite link carries your name, username and answers after the “#” in the link. Browsers don’t send that part to the website’s server.
  • Your collection is not sent to the server. Prices and places never go to the community server.

The account service is hosted by Supabase, and sign-in emails are sent through Resend. The website memorywax.app is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like most hosts, they may keep ordinary network logs, such as your IP address, which we don’t see.

What we don’t do

  • No advertising. No analytics or tracking code in the app. We don’t sell data.
  • We don’t collect your location. The “Remember where and when I scan” setting records nothing today.

Your choices

  • Take it with you: Settings → “Download a backup” (everything, with photos) or “Export as CSV”. A backup file contains your prices, places and photos, so treat it as private. It never contains your Discogs token.
  • Turn off sync: Settings → “Sync with iCloud”.
  • Pause all sharing: Settings → “Pause all sharing”.
  • Delete your community account: Community account → “Delete my account” removes your sign-in, username, profile, friendships, blocks and your etching reports from the server for good. If you signed in with Apple, the app also asks Apple to disconnect Memory Wax from your Apple Account. Our hosts may keep backups for a short while after deletion, and the safety copies of reports described above remain for the period stated.
  • Erase this phone: Settings → “Erase everything on this phone” removes your records, photos and tokens, and signs you out. Sync turns off on that phone and your other devices keep their records. Deleting the app removes its data from the phone, but not what’s in your iCloud.
  • Camera and microphone: you can refuse either. You can type a barcode or search by name instead, and Listen simply won’t work.

Children

Memory Wax isn’t for children under 13. The app asks you to confirm you are 13 or older before you make an account, and we delete an account if we learn it belongs to a younger child. We don’t knowingly collect their data.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, we’ll say so in the app and update the date above. Before anything new is collected, such as friends seeing your shelves, we’ll update this page first.
